## 1. Role Summary

The Global Freight Specialist owns the end-to-end logistics chain that gets completed vehicles off the production floor in Melbourne and delivered to port of destination — starting now with Jeddah Islamic Port (King Abdulaziz Port), Saudi Arabia. This is not a transport coordinator role and not a customs broker role, though it overlaps both. It is a specialist freight function requiring deep operational knowledge of Roll-on Roll-off (RoRo) shipping, high-and-heavy cargo handling, Australian export compliance, and Saudi Arabian import requirements for commercial vehicles.

The Works produces prime movers and fuel tankers — completed, fully built heavy vehicles. These are not standard automotive units. They are high-and-heavy cargo: long, wide, over-height relative to standard car-carrier lanes, and potentially carrying residual dangerous goods product in tank vessels. The person in this role must be able to book and manage RoRo shipments for this cargo class specifically — not general freight or containerised cargo.

The immediate requirement is the Melbourne → Jeddah lane. No direct RoRo service operates on this route; transhipment via Singapore, Port Klang, or Jebel Ali is the working assumption. The Freight Specialist selects the optimal routing, negotiates with carriers, manages Australian export documentation, interfaces with Saudi customs agents, and coordinates biosecurity clearance. They do so with cost minimisation as the primary objective, not speed.

Volumes are initially low (1–10 units per shipment) but the role must be structured to scale. The person must understand the cost levers at both carrier and port level, and maintain active relationships with carriers operating on the Australia–Middle East RoRo lane.

## 3. Day-to-Day Tasks

- Obtaining and comparing freight rates from active H&H RoRo carriers (WWL, Höegh, K-Line, MOL, Grimaldi) for Melbourne departure to Jeddah via available transhipment hubs
- Evaluating routing options (direct call availability vs. transhipment penalties vs. schedule reliability) and presenting cost/time options to MEO
- Booking confirmed cargo space with the selected carrier; issuing booking instructions; managing cut-off dates for documentation, customs, and vessel arrival
- Coordinating DAFF biosecurity inspection: scheduling vehicle presentation, ensuring cleaning requirements are met prior to inspection, obtaining phytosanitary clearance
- Lodging Australian export entry via the ABF Integrated Cargo System; preparing and issuing the full export documentation set (Commercial Invoice, Packing List, Bill of Lading instructions, Certificate of Origin where required)
- Briefing the Saudi port agent ahead of each shipment: issuing arrival notification, pre-clearing customs documentation, confirming power of attorney and customs bond arrangements
- Interfacing with Remy to determine DG classification status of each fuel tanker shipment — whether a gas-free certificate is required and what IMDG documentation must travel with the vessel
- Monitoring vessel schedules from Port of Melbourne through transhipment to Jeddah; managing transhipment cut-offs; alerting MEO to delays or missed connections
- Tracking customs clearance status at Jeddah Islamic Port through the Saudi port agent; managing demurrage risk; escalating if ZATCA or SASO hold up clearance
- Reviewing freight invoices against agreed rates; disputing surcharges not in the booking confirmation
- Maintaining a lane cost model: tracking freight rate movements on the Australia–Middle East RoRo lane, identifying optimal booking windows, flagging rate spikes or capacity constraints
- Keeping carrier relationship active between shipments: maintaining service contracts or rate agreements where volume justifies it
- Advising MEO on Incoterm selection for each shipment based on the owner's commercial arrangement with the Saudi buyer

## 5. Typical Credentials

**Formal Qualifications**

- Degree or diploma in Logistics, Supply Chain, International Trade, or Maritime Studies — or equivalent industry experience
- Diploma of International Freight Forwarding (TLI50219) or Certificate IV in Customs Broking (FNS41019) — either is directly relevant; both is a strong signal
- Membership of the Freight & Trade Alliance (FTA) or Customs Brokers and Forwarders Council of Australia (CBFCA) — indicates active engagement with the regulatory landscape

**Experience Benchmarks**

- Minimum 5 years in international freight forwarding with hands-on RoRo or project cargo experience — not general ocean freight
- Demonstrated experience booking and managing high-and-heavy RoRo cargo (prime movers, construction equipment, mining plant, or similar oversized wheeled cargo)
- Demonstrated experience with the Australia–Middle East freight lane; Saudi Arabia import experience strongly preferred over generic Middle East experience
- Experience interfacing directly with ABF, DAFF biosecurity, and customs agents — not delegating all compliance to a third-party forwarder
- Active carrier relationships with at least two of: WWL, Höegh Autoliners, K-Line, MOL, Grimaldi

**Desirable but Not Required**

- Licensed Australian customs broker (would remove the need to engage a third-party broker for export entries)
- Arabic language competency (useful for direct Saudi agent relationships)
- Experience with Saudi SASO type approval process for commercial vehicles
- Project cargo or heavy-lift experience beyond standard H&H RoRo (for potential future shipments of non-self-propelled cargo or plant equipment)
- Exposure to IMDG DG documentation for tanker cargo at sea

## 6. Sub-Specialisations — Worth Noting

**High & Heavy vs. Automotive RoRo**
Most RoRo freight experience sits on the automotive side (car carriers, new vehicle logistics). That is a different market with different carrier relationships, different terminals, and different rate structures. The Freight Specialist must come from the H&H side — prime movers, buses, construction plant, agricultural equipment. Automotive experience alone is disqualifying. Screen for this explicitly.

**Project Cargo / Break-Bulk Crossover**
Some candidates will have a background in project cargo (heavy-lift, out-of-gauge, ODC — out-of-dimension cargo) alongside RoRo. This crossover is valuable if the owner ever needs to ship non-self-propelled chassis or plant that cannot roll under its own power. A H&H RoRo specialist who also understands flat-rack or mafi trailer operations adds genuine optionality.

**Saudi Arabia / Gulf Market Specialists**
The Middle East is a niche lane from Australia. Candidates with specific Saudi or Gulf experience — ZATCA clearance, SASO product conformity, Saudi port agent networks at Jeddah, Jubail, Dammam — are significantly more valuable than a general Asia-Pacific freight specialist. If a candidate has done multiple Saudi heavy vehicle shipments, they bring ready-made agent relationships and regulatory pathway knowledge that otherwise takes months to build.

**Dangerous Goods by Sea (IMDG)**
Candidates with an IMDG DG-by-sea background and practical experience classifying cargo with residual product will meaningfully reduce the coordination overhead on the Remy interface. This is a genuine differentiator for the tanker shipment cycle, where the DG classification question arises every time a vessel leaves production.

## 7. Felix's Notes

**What this person does NOT do**

- Does not assess whether a fuel tanker is compliant with Australian DG legislation or the ADG Code — that is Remy's domain.
- Does not design or assess the structural integrity of any vehicle — that is Harlow's domain.
- Does not determine whether a vehicle meets Saudi road registration standards for ongoing operation in-country — that is a Saudi Ministry of Transport matter, handled by the owner's Saudi customer or agent.
- Does not perform customs brokerage on the Saudi side unless they hold the relevant qualification — the Saudi agent performs in-country customs lodgement.
- Does not manage domestic freight from the production floor to Port of Melbourne — that is a separate local transport coordination function, either handled by this person at a basic level or contracted to a local carrier.

**Interface mapping**

1. Freight Specialist → Remy: Before each tanker shipment, the Freight Specialist must confirm DG classification status with Remy. Remy determines whether the vessel qualifies as DG cargo under the IMDG Code, what gas-free or cleaning certification is required, and what documentation must accompany the shipment. The Freight Specialist then executes the carrier notification and documentation — Remy does not interface with carriers directly.

2. Freight Specialist → Harlow: The Freight Specialist requires accurate vehicle dimensions, gross vehicle mass (GVM), and driven/towed status for each unit before booking. Harlow is the source of this data. Dimensional inaccuracies at the carrier booking stage create stowage failures and surcharges at the port.

3. Freight Specialist → MEO: Cost options and routing recommendations are presented to MEO for decision before committing to a carrier booking. MEO does not receive carrier paperwork — it receives a structured options comparison and a recommendation. Once MEO approves, the Freight Specialist executes without further escalation unless a material change arises.

**Hiring priority signal**

The absence of this role is currently blocking the owner's ability to export completed builds. The production capability exists; the logistics channel does not. This hire unlocks revenue. It is not supplementary to existing operations — it enables the first completed shipment. Priority is high and the brief should be treated as time-sensitive.

**Note on customs broker vs. freight forwarder**

The owner may be tempted to engage a general-purpose freight forwarder who subcontracts to a customs broker and a RoRo carrier. That arrangement adds cost and removes the direct carrier relationship that drives cost efficiency on a low-volume, high-value lane. A specialist who can negotiate directly with carriers and interface with ABF without an intermediary — even if they use a Saudi agent at destination — is strongly preferred.

**Suggested screening question for Nolan**

Ask candidates: "Walk me through the last high-and-heavy RoRo shipment you personally booked from an Australian port to a Middle East destination — what carrier did you use, what was the routing, what was the approximate freight rate basis (per linear metre or per unit), and what was the hardest compliance problem you had to solve to get the cargo on the vessel."

A candidate who has done this recently will answer in specifics. A candidate who has general freight experience but no H&H RoRo on this lane will generalise. The answer makes the distinction immediately.
