## 1. Role Summary

A Process and Instrumentation Engineer (P&IE) specialising in LPG and dangerous goods road tanker systems designs, documents, and certifies the fluid process systems mounted on road tankers — pumps, valves, pipework, instrumentation, and control systems. This person is the author and custodian of P&ID drawings. They sit at the intersection of process engineering, instrumentation engineering, and dangerous goods compliance. On a road tanker, they own everything from the tank outlet to the discharge nozzle, including all vapour management, spray fill, bypass, and relief systems.

---

## 2. Core Knowledge Domains

| Domain | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| P&ID authoring and review | Creation, revision, and interpretation of Piping and Instrumentation Diagrams to ISA 5.1 / AS 1101.6 standards; symbol conventions; line numbering; instrument tagging |
| LPG fluid systems | LPG physical properties (vapour pressure, flash point, two-phase flow); pump types for LPG (positive displacement, centrifugal); cavitation and flashing at valve restrictions; vapour recovery and return systems |
| Dangerous goods road tanker systems | AS 3788 (pressure equipment); AS 2030 (gas cylinder equipment); AS 1596 (LPG storage and handling); ADG Code Class 2.1 requirements; WorkCover/SafeWork dangerous goods vehicle approval processes |
| Valve and instrumentation specification | ISC valves, bypass valves, differential valves, pressure relief valves, excess flow valves, check valves; pneumatic actuators; instrumentation (pressure gauges, flow meters, differential pressure devices) |
| Spray fill and vapour systems | LPG spray fill systems; differential pressure control; vapour bypass return; recirculation loop hydraulics; restriction orifice sizing |
| Hydraulic/pneumatic system analysis | Pressure drop calculations; flow rate calculations; orifice sizing (ISO 5167 or equivalent); pump curve analysis; system curve matching |
| Regulatory compliance | Victorian WorkCover Authority dangerous goods vehicle approval; NHVR heavy vehicle requirements; AS/NZS standards for LPG; DG Act notification and modification approval processes |
| Hazardous area classification | AS/NZS 60079 (explosive atmospheres); zone classification around LPG equipment; equipment selection for hazardous areas |
| Pressure equipment | Design registration; inspection and testing of pressure vessels and piping (AS 4458, AS 4041); hydrostatic and pneumatic testing |

---

## 3. Day-to-Day Tasks

- Authoring and revising P&ID drawings to ISA standards
- Reviewing as-built discrepancies and issuing drawing revisions
- Sizing restriction orifices, control valves, and relief devices
- Writing and reviewing operating procedures for dangerous goods systems
- Preparing and submitting WorkCover/SafeWork modification documentation
- Specifying valves, instruments, and fittings for LPG service
- Conducting hydraulic calculations for flow, pressure drop, and differential pressure
- Analysing system faults using P&ID and operational data
- Liaising with WorkCover inspectors and authorised inspecting bodies
- Reviewing fabricator and body builder drawings for process compliance

## 7. Felix's Notes

This role is compliance-heavy. The critical credential for the Australian context is CPEng or equivalent — without it, the engineer cannot sign off on modifications to WorkCover-registered dangerous goods plant. The LPG knowledge is learnable from a strong mechanical/chemical engineer, but the regulatory pathway (WorkCover, DG Act, pressure equipment registration) takes time and is non-negotiable for this domain.

The immediate task (P&ID review, orifice sizing, operating procedure change on a WorkCover-approved LPG tanker) requires someone who can interact directly with WorkCover Victoria and author revision documentation.
