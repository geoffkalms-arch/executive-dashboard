## 1. Role Summary

This is not a generalist compliance role. The Compliance Specialist is the team's authority on regulatory obligations that arise when physical modifications are made to registered dangerous goods plant mounted on heavy vehicles operating in Australian road transport. The person must be able to navigate the intersection of three overlapping regulatory regimes simultaneously: dangerous goods legislation (Commonwealth and Victorian), heavy vehicle road transport law (NHVR and ADRs), and occupational health and safety/plant approval frameworks (WorkCover Victoria and equivalents in other states).

The team's work generates compliance obligations at every stage — concept, design, modification, commissioning, and ongoing operation. The Compliance Specialist interprets those obligations, ensures the team's deliverables satisfy them, manages approval pathways, and maintains the documentary record required by regulators. They are a translator between engineering intent and regulatory requirement, not an engineer themselves.

The role is high-consequence. Vehicles carry Class 2.1 (LPG), Class 3 (flammable liquids), Class 6 (toxic substances), and Class 8 (corrosive substances). An approval gap on a registered DG plant is not a paperwork problem — it is a legal liability and a public safety exposure.

## 2. Core Knowledge Domains

| Domain | What They Must Know |
|---|---|
| ADG Code 7th Edition | Division and class requirements for road tankers; construction, marking, placarding, documentation, and driver obligations; how the Code interacts with state legislation |
| AS 2809 Series | Road tank vehicles for dangerous goods — construction standards for tanks, valves, fittings, and equipment; knows which parts of the series apply to which vehicle types |
| Australian Design Rules (ADRs) | Which ADRs apply to heavy vehicles post-modification; when a modification triggers re-certification; interface with NHVR modification standards |
| NHVR Modification Code | PBS scheme, modification approval pathways, accredited vehicle examiner (AVE) process; what constitutes a notifiable modification vs. a prescribed modification |
| WorkCover Victoria — Plant Approval | Registration of dangerous goods plant; what physical changes require re-approval; how to structure a change submission; as-built drawing requirements; interface with the Equipment (Public Safety) Act (Vic) |
| Victorian Dangerous Goods Act 1998 | Licensing, storage and handling, transport compliance, duty holder obligations |
| Management of Change (MOC) | MOC process as applied to registered plant; when MOC triggers re-approval vs. records update only; documentation standards |
| Chain of Responsibility (CoR) | Heavy Vehicle National Law CoR obligations; duty holder mapping across the transport chain; how engineering changes affect CoR exposure for the team and the operator |
| AS 1596 | Storage and handling of LP Gas; interface with vehicle-mounted LPG systems |
| AS/NZS 60079 | Explosive atmospheres — equipment classification; when this standard governs equipment selection on DG vehicles |
| NMI (Trade Measurement) | When NMI compliance applies to metering equipment on road tankers; interface with NTMA pattern approval and meter certification |
| Interoperability of State/Territory DG regimes | Where Victorian obligations align with or diverge from other jurisdictions; cross-border operation implications |

## 3. Day-to-Day Tasks

- Review proposed P&ID changes, subframe modifications, and PTO/pump installations at the concept stage to identify approval obligations before engineering work commences.
- Determine whether a proposed physical modification to registered plant requires WorkCover re-approval, NHVR modification approval, both, or neither — and advise the team accordingly.
- Manage the WorkCover Victoria plant re-approval process: compile change submissions, coordinate as-built drawing packages, liaise with the regulator, track approval status.
- Maintain the MOC register for all modifications to registered DG plant — document the change, the regulatory assessment, the approval pathway taken, and the approval outcome.
- Review as-built drawings against approved plant documentation; identify and resolve discrepancies before they become regulatory exposure.
- Map CoR obligations when the team's engineering recommendations affect how vehicles are operated, loaded, or maintained — advise on how operators should document and manage those obligations.
- Draft compliance summaries for client-facing deliverables, flagging the approval steps the operator must complete.
- Monitor regulatory change: amendments to the ADG Code, WorkCover plant approval policy, NHVR modification standards, and relevant AS standards.
- Advise on placarding, marking, and documentation requirements for modified vehicles.
- Identify when a proposed modification would take a vehicle outside the scope of its current ADR certification and escalate to the appropriate vehicle certification pathway.

## 5. Typical Credentials

**Highly regarded:**
- Tertiary qualification in occupational health and safety, engineering (any discipline), or law — with significant post-qualification experience in dangerous goods or heavy vehicle compliance.
- Certificate IV or Diploma in Work Health and Safety, combined with specialist DG experience.
- Formal training in the ADG Code (many providers offer this; ALSC and similar transport safety organisations run recognised programs).
- Accredited Vehicle Examiner (AVE) status under NHVR — highly valuable but not a requirement; this is a strength if held.

**Practical experience outweighs paper credentials in this role.** A candidate with ten years managing WorkCover plant approvals and DG transport compliance for a tanker operator or an OEM, without a degree, is more useful than a compliance generalist with qualifications but no heavy vehicle DG exposure.

**Desirable experience markers:**
- Has personally lodged a WorkCover plant re-approval submission for a modified road tanker.
- Has managed an MOC process on registered DG plant through to approval.
- Has dealt with the NHVR modification approval pathway for a heavy vehicle modification.
- Has read and applied AS 2809 in a practical modification context — not just cited it.
- Knows the difference between what WorkCover Victoria requires and what the ADG Code requires, and when those obligations diverge.

## 7. Felix's Notes

**What this role is not:**

This person is not an engineer. They do not design, specify, or assess the structural or mechanical adequacy of modifications. That is Harlow's domain (structural and body engineering) and Petra's domain (P&ID, instrumentation, process engineering). The Compliance Specialist reads the engineering output and determines what the regulatory system requires of it — they do not produce the engineering output.

Specifically:
- They do not determine whether a subframe modification is structurally adequate — Harlow does.
- They do not assess whether a P&ID change is technically sound for the process — Petra does.
- They do not select equipment on technical grounds — they advise on whether the selected equipment meets regulatory classification requirements (e.g., AS/NZS 60079 zone classification).

**Where they must interface tightly:**

The MOC process is the critical integration point. When Harlow or Petra proposes a change to registered plant, the Compliance Specialist must be in the loop immediately — before drawings are progressed — to determine the approval pathway. Late compliance engagement is one of the highest-risk patterns in DG plant modification. This person needs to be upstream, not downstream.

**Recruitment flag:**

The overlap between WorkCover plant approval and DG transport regulation is a narrow niche in Australia. The candidate pool is small. Nolan should expect to consider candidates from OEM workshops, DG tanker operators' internal compliance functions, and specialist transport safety consultancies — not from generalist compliance or WHS backgrounds. Someone who has spent time in the petroleum transport sector or LPG distribution is likely to have the closest fit. Victoria-based experience is preferable given the WorkCover Victoria interface, but a strong candidate with equivalent interstate experience in plant approval (SafeWork NSW, Workplace Health and Safety Queensland) is worth considering given the ADG Code and NHVR frameworks are national.
