## 1. Role Summary

The Balanced Scorecard Implementation Specialist designs, builds, and maintains BSC frameworks for engineering and manufacturing businesses in the transport sector. This person translates organisational strategy into measurable objectives across all four BSC perspectives, cascades those objectives to team and individual level, and ensures that operational outputs from the engineering team (Harlow, Petra, Remy) feed into scorecard metrics in a form that informs management decisions.

This is an implementation and maintenance role — not a strategy consultant and not a financial analyst. The specialist works from strategy that has already been set and makes it visible, measurable, and actionable. In a transport engineering and manufacturing context, that means connecting things like vehicle build throughput, process uptime, defect rates, and compliance status to the strategic picture the owner sees on the executive dashboard.

The role is internally facing. Primary relationships are with MEO (who sets direction), the engineering team members (who generate operational data), and the owner (who reads the output).

## 2. Core Knowledge Domains

| Domain | What Is Required | Depth |
|---|---|---|
| BSC methodology | Full four-perspective framework: financial, customer, internal process, learning & growth — including how they interact | Expert |
| Strategy mapping | Cause-and-effect mapping between strategic objectives; translating vision into strategy maps | Expert |
| KPI design | Defining lead and lag indicators; selecting measures that are actionable, not merely available | Expert |
| KPI cascade | Breaking top-level KPIs into team-level and role-level measures that remain aligned and non-contradictory | Advanced |
| Manufacturing operations | Production throughput, OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness), defect rates, cycle times, yield — specifically in a fabrication or vehicle build context | Solid working knowledge |
| Transport sector context | Heavy vehicle lifecycle, fleet build schedules, regulatory touchpoints (ADR compliance, chain of responsibility), workshop and field service cycles | Solid working knowledge |
| Performance reporting | Cadence design (daily operational, weekly team, monthly executive, quarterly strategic), report structure, and audience calibration | Expert |
| OKR integration | Where OKRs complement or overlap BSC; how to run both without duplication or conflict | Working knowledge |
| Process instrumentation data | Ability to read outputs from P&ID-based monitoring, SCADA summaries, or process logs and convert them into scorecard-ready metrics | Working knowledge |
| Change management (light) | Enough to embed a new performance framework in a team that has not previously used one; driving adoption without a dedicated change manager | Competent |

## 3. Day-to-Day Tasks

- Maintain the live scorecard — update metrics each reporting period, flag exceptions, escalate to MEO where thresholds are breached
- Meet with Harlow, Petra, and Remy each cycle to collect operational data, clarify anomalies, and validate that the numbers represent what actually happened
- Translate Harlow's vehicle body build outputs (build completion rates, rework hours, materials waste) into Internal Process and Financial perspective metrics
- Translate Petra's process and instrumentation data (uptime, cycle time, process deviations, energy consumption) into Internal Process metrics and, where relevant, Learning & Growth targets
- Translate Remy's compliance status (audit outcomes, incident counts, licence and certification currency, DG plant compliance) into Customer and Internal Process perspective measures
- Maintain strategy maps — update when strategy shifts, not on a fixed calendar
- Design and run the quarterly strategic review cadence: prepare the pack, facilitate the session, update the scorecard post-session
- Identify KPIs that are lagging in signal quality (too late, too coarse, or not acted upon) and propose replacements
- Build and maintain the data dictionary — what each metric means, how it is calculated, who owns the source data, and how frequently it refreshes
- Produce the executive dashboard input — structured, timestamped, source-attributed, consistent with the team's data standards
- Onboard new team members to scorecard logic when the team grows

## 4. Tools, Frameworks, and Standards

| Category | Specific Tools / Frameworks / Standards | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| BSC methodology | Kaplan & Norton BSC framework (original and second-generation); Palladium Group execution model | The Kaplan-Norton canon is non-negotiable baseline |
| Strategy mapping | Kaplan & Norton strategy map structure; Balanced Scorecard Institute templates | Must be able to build and maintain maps without a consultant |
| KPI frameworks | SMART KPI design; lead vs. lag indicator discipline; KPI library management | Avoid vanity metrics — this team is small and each metric must earn its place |
| OKR integration | OKR methodology (Google / Doerr model) | For teams that run OKRs alongside BSC; must be able to distinguish and reconcile |
| Scorecard software | Preferred: ClearPoint Strategy, Corporater, or BST Global; acceptable: Power BI or Tableau as reporting layer with manual scorecard backend; Excel-based where appropriate for small team | Software should serve the framework, not define it |
| Manufacturing metrics | OEE (Availability × Performance × Quality); TEEP; Six Sigma DMAIC as a metric source (not as a practitioner role) | Heavy vehicle body manufacturing context |
| Reporting standards | ISO 9001 (quality management outputs as metric inputs); internal report style consistent with The Works data standards | Rule 3 (timestamped data) applies to all outputs |
| Data integration | Ability to pull from spreadsheets, simple databases, SCADA exports, and ERP extracts — not a data engineer, but must not be blocked by structured data | Petra's process outputs and Remy's compliance registers are likely sources |
| Project tracking | Enough Jira, Asana, or equivalent to read team-level work status and convert it to L&G perspective metrics | Read access, not administration |

## 5. Typical Credentials

**Formal qualifications — one or more expected:**
- Bachelor's degree in Business, Engineering Management, Industrial Engineering, or Operations Management
- Postgraduate qualification (MBA, Graduate Certificate) with a strategy or operations focus is a strong positive signal but not required
- Balanced Scorecard Professional (BSP) certification from the Balanced Scorecard Institute — the most directly relevant credential; a candidate without it should have demonstrably equivalent applied experience
- Lean Six Sigma Green Belt or above — not required, but common in candidates who have worked in manufacturing environments and indicates comfort with process metrics

**Experience signals that matter more than credentials:**
- Has built at least one BSC from scratch (not just maintained an inherited one) in a manufacturing or industrial environment
- Has personally designed KPI cascades that reached the team and individual level — not just C-suite scorecards
- Has presented scorecard results to a principal or executive audience and defended metric choices under scrutiny
- Has worked directly with engineers or tradespeople to source operational data — not solely with finance or strategy teams
- Familiarity with Australian regulatory environment (ADR, NHVR, WHS) as a compliance metric context is a genuine differentiator in this team

## 7. Felix's Notes

**On the boundary of this role.** This is the clearest thing Nolan needs to hold in the profile. The BSC Specialist does not set strategy — MEO does that in dialogue with the owner. The specialist does not analyse financial statements — they read financial metrics that already exist and place them in the Financial perspective. They do not build data pipelines — they consume outputs that Petra, Harlow, and Remy already produce. If a candidate's pitch is "I can tell you what your strategy should be" or "I can build you a data warehouse," they are the wrong person.

**On the engineering team interface.** This is the live tension in the role. Harlow, Petra, and Remy produce operational data in engineering terms — build hours, process deviations, compliance audit counts. The BSC Specialist must be able to sit with each of them, understand what the number actually means in context, and translate it without distortion into a scorecard metric. That requires enough manufacturing literacy to not be dismissed by the engineering team and enough strategic literacy to not get lost in the operational detail. The ideal candidate has worked on a shop floor or in a plant environment at some point, even briefly — it changes how they ask questions.

**On the Australian transport context.** The team operates in a regulated environment. Remy's compliance outputs include NHVR chain of responsibility, ADR certification, and dangerous goods plant compliance. The BSC Specialist needs to understand what a compliance breach looks like as a scorecard event — not just a red cell on a dashboard, but an escalation trigger with defined response. Candidates with experience in Australian transport, mining, or resources sectors will have seen this kind of regulatory metric in a performance management context. Candidates from pure corporate or financial services BSC backgrounds will need a calibration period.

**On tooling.** Do not let the software choice dominate the interview. A strong candidate will have a view on tooling but will not be dependent on a specific platform. The framework knowledge is what matters. The Works' executive dashboard is already structured around timestamped, source-attributed data (per Rule 3) — the BSC Specialist needs to operate within that discipline, whatever tool sits underneath.

**On seniority.** Given the team is small and this person will be operating with significant autonomy — no BSC team above them, no strategy office to defer to — this should not be a junior hire. A candidate with fewer than five years of applied BSC implementation experience in an industrial environment is unlikely to have the independent judgment the role requires. The floor is mid-level; senior is preferable.
